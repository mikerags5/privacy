Privacy — SimpleHT

Last updated 20 September 2026.

SimpleHT programs a TIDRADIO TD-H3 handheld radio from an iPhone or iPad.

It collects nothing, and it cannot: the app makes no network requests at all. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising identifier, no crash-reporting service and no third-party SDK of any kind. URLSession does not appear anywhere in the source. The only thing it talks to is a radio, over Bluetooth, and the whole of that conversation is the eight kilobytes of memory the radio holds.

That claim is checkable rather than asserted: the app ships an Apple privacy manifest (PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy) declaring no data collection and no tracking, and the source is available on request. The repository is private today because it contains dumps of the author's own radio; if that changes, it will be linked here.

This text is also inside the app, under About and privacy on the home page, so it is readable without a network and without this page being reachable.

What stays on your device

Everything. Specifically:

  • Configurations — the channel line-ups you save, and the recoverable copy of whatever you are working on.
  • Channel Sets — the groups of channels you build and reuse.
  • Backups — a copy of a radio's memory taken before every write, so a half-written radio has something to go back to, plus the journal that records what was sent.
  • Radio nicknames — a name you give a handset so two of them can be told apart. It never leaves the phone and is never included in a shared file.

All of it lives in the app's own storage. Deleting the app deletes all of it, and nothing is copied anywhere first.

What leaves your device, and only when you ask

The share sheet. You can share one configuration, one Channel Set, or one backup at a time, as a file, to whoever you choose — and where it goes is entirely your choice, because the app has no idea what the share sheet does with it. A shared backup is two files: the radio image, byte for byte, and a sidecar describing when and how it was taken — with the identifier and the name you gave the radio withheld, which the sidecar says rather than leaving blank. The image carries whatever the radio held, including the channel names you programmed, and the screen you share from says so.

A shared file carries no Bluetooth identifier and no nickname. A configuration read from a radio records which radio it came from so the app can suggest that radio as the target later; that identifier is stripped before the file is written. The test that proves it checks the encoded bytes rather than the object, so a field added in a later version cannot quietly start carrying it.

A shared file contains the channels — frequencies, names, tones, power, bandwidth — the mode the configuration was made for, and the radio model. That is all.

Bluetooth

The app asks for Bluetooth because it is the only way to reach the radio. It scans for TD-H3 handsets, reads the one you tap, and writes to the one you choose. It does not use Bluetooth for anything else, has no background mode, and stops scanning when you leave the screen.

Children

There is nothing here directed at children and nothing collected from anyone.
